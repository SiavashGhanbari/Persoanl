When money is late, say when: Bitpin's toman withdrawals

Our bank closed the instant payout channel. Withdrawals that used to land in seconds now took hours, and users read the silence as lost money.

Context

Bitpin users were used to instant toman withdrawals: tap withdraw, and the money is in their bank account a moment later.

Then the bank shut down our instant payment channel. Every withdrawal had to go through the bank's PAYA batch cycle, which can take a few hours or up to a day. Nothing in the app changed to reflect that.

The problem

  • 1
    An expectation the product could no longer keep. Users expected money in seconds. When it didn't arrive, they assumed something had gone wrong.
  • 2
    No visibility after tapping withdraw. The app showed "submitted" and nothing else. Users had no way to tell a normal delay from a real problem, so they called support or opened a ticket.

About 40% of toman withdrawal tickets were some version of "Where is my money and why hasn't it arrived?". The rest were genuine system or bank issues, which are out of scope here.

The flow

From silence to a clear timeline

    The solution

    1. Set the expectation before the tap. A snackbar in the withdrawal flow tells users exactly when the money will arrive: "Withdraw now and it lands in your account by 16:30."

    2. Show where the money is. A new withdrawal details page tracks every step: request received, security review, regulatory (FATA) check, sent to the bank, bank confirmation with an exact deposit time, and deposited.

    3. Tell users when to worry, and only then. We can't see when the bank actually deposits the money. So if the promised time passes and it still hasn't arrived, the page invites the user to contact support. Before that, there's nothing to worry about, and the page says so.

      Results

      Toman withdrawal tickets dropped by 35%. Since about 40% of tickets were "where is my money" questions, that means the large majority of them (roughly 35 of every 40) simply stopped coming in.

      What I learned

      We couldn't make the money faster. The bank decided that. What we could control was what users knew while they waited. A clear time and a visible next step turned a scary silence into a normal wait.
