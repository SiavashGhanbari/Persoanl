Paid is not purchased: fixing Zarpin's gold checkout

Zarpin lets 400K+ users buy gold from their phone. The app looked polished, but the buy flow had two places where users thought they were done when they weren't.

Context

When I joined, the product was designed around visuals and concept rather than the flows that drive revenue. My first move was to shift the team's attention to the core journeys and the pain points inside them.

The gold purchase flow was the most important one, and the data showed users leaking at two specific steps.

The problem

  • 1
    A pre-invoice that looked final. It was a full page that read like a receipt. Users thought the purchase was done and left, or got stuck on it.
  • 2
    A hidden "Complete purchase" step. After paying, the app said "Deposit successful" and showed a button. If users didn't tap it, they only had rials in their wallet and no gold.

The flow

From five steps with two traps, to four clear ones

    The solution

    1. The pre-invoice became a bottom sheet. It sits on top of the buy screen, so it reads as a step, not an ending. It says clearly that it is not the final invoice, and it is a shared component we can drop into any flow where a user buys gold.

    2. Payment and purchase became one moment. After the gateway, the success screen says "Your deposit arrived, we're buying your gold now" and completes the purchase automatically. The user understands what's happening and doesn't need to do anything else.

    What I learned

    The most expensive bugs in a checkout are not visual. They are moments where the interface says "done" too early. Naming each state honestly (pre-invoice, payment, purchase) did more for conversion than any visual polish.
