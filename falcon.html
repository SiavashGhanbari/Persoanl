Falcon · Internal accounting tool

From a 3-hour paper list to one click: Falcon's settlement planner

Falcon was the in-house accounting and trading software of a currency exchange in Turkey, built so trade data could stay inside the company. Every evening, traders spent hours planning the next day's rial settlements by hand.

Context

Not every trade settled on the day it was made. A trader might sell dollars for rials and agree to settle in five days, at the customer's request or ours. So on top of the day's trades, every day also had a list of settlements that came due.

For rials this was constant, so it was where the work piled up.

The problem

At the end of each day, traders sat down and wrote out, on paper, every settlement due tomorrow: the amount, the time and the payment method. The next day they messaged each customer on WhatsApp, one by one, to send the money.

  • 1
    Matching debtors to creditors by hand. If customer A owed us money and we owed customers B and C, traders didn't collect from A and pay out again. They told A to pay B and C directly, building a chain of settlements in their heads.
  • 2
    Every customer had rules. Person or company, a settlement window (morning, noon or afternoon, because of bank transfer limits), and specific source or destination accounts. All of it had to be respected when pairing people up.
  • 3
    Tired people, after hours. Building the list took 2–3 hours after the working day. Traders were paid overtime for it, and still made 2–3 mistakes a day, some of which turned into real losses or extra costs.

The flow

From an evening of paperwork to a reviewed list

    What I did

    I spotted the problem, proposed the feature and made the case for it in terms of cost: overtime, errors and the losses they caused.

    1. Turn tribal knowledge into data. We added each customer's rules to Falcon as structured fields: person or company, settlement window, and allowed source and destination accounts. Collecting this for every active customer took about a month.

    2. Generate the list in one click. Falcon finds everything due tomorrow, pairs debtors with creditors within each settlement window, and hands the trader a ready list: who pays whom, how much, when, and to which account.

    3. Keep a human in the loop on purpose. We could have sent the WhatsApp messages automatically too. We chose not to: a trader reviews each instruction and sends it, so a wrong account number is caught before money moves.

            Traders went home on time, the company stopped paying overtime for list building, and daily settlement errors went from 2–3 a day to zero, along with the losses and extra fees they used to cause.

            What I learned

            The feature itself was simple. The hard part was seeing that a routine everyone accepted as "just part of the job" was a product problem with a real cost. And full automation isn't always the goal: keeping one human check where money moves was the right call.
